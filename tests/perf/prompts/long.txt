You are writing an internal engineering memo. The audience is a team of systems engineers who have deep experience in C, Linux, and CPU micro-architecture, but limited familiarity with large language model inference. Your task is to explain, in a single memo of moderate length and precise technical language, why a correctness-first CPU-only local inference stack is a reasonable starting point before any SIMD, GPU, or distributed work is considered. Cover at least the following dimensions in your explanation, with concrete examples where possible:

1. The role of a reference implementation as the authoritative correctness oracle against which all future optimized backends must compare.
2. The separation of prefill and decode as two distinct execution regimes with different compute, memory, and parallelism characteristics.
3. Why static batching is the right initial target even though production systems ultimately want continuous batching or paged attention.
4. The context window as an explicit architectural policy rather than a loose runtime option, and the reasons that conflating trained maximum context with configured context leads to subtle bugs in KV cache allocation, attention masking, and RoPE frequency tables.
5. The specific reasons plain RoPE is handled first, and why Llama 3 style extended RoPE, YaRN, and long-context schemes should be explicitly deferred rather than silently supported.
6. The discipline of keeping every newly added .c file paired with a docs explanation so that the project remains navigable to a future coding agent or new team member.
7. Performance measurement as a separate, opt-in surface layered onto the correctness baseline so that timing instrumentation cannot accidentally change inference outputs.

Close the memo with three concrete, actionable recommendations for the team to follow in the coming two weeks, and end with a short note inviting questions. Keep the tone direct, technical, and free of marketing language.
